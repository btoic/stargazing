# Equipment: Sky-Watcher Skyliner Classic 200P Dobsonian

## 1. Telescope Specifications
- **Model**: Sky-Watcher Skyliner Classic 200P Dobsonian
- **Optical Design**: Newtonian Reflector
- **Mount Type**: Alt-Azimuth Dobsonian (manual push-to / star-hopping)
- **Aperture ($D$)**: `200 mm` (7.87 inches)
- **Focal Length ($F$)**: `1200 mm`
- **Focal Ratio**: `f/6` (Moderate focal ratio; minimal coma, forgiving on eyepiece collimation)
- **Theoretical Resolving Power (Dawes Limit)**: `0.58 arcseconds` ($\frac{116}{D_{\text{mm}}}$)
- **Visual Limiting Magnitude**: `13.3 – 14.0 mag`
- **Light Gathering Capacity**: `820×` naked human eye (7 mm dark-adapted pupil)
- **Maximum Useful Magnification**: `~400×` (typically 150×–200× in typical atmospheric seeing)
- **Optical Tube**: Solid steel tube, length 1150 mm, tube weight ~11 kg, base weight ~12.5 kg
- **Focuser**: 2" Crayford focuser with 1.25" adapter and tension control

## 3. Optical Inversion & Star-Hopping Rules
- **Newtonian Reflector Eyepiece View**:
  - The view through the eyepiece is **rotated 180°** (inverted up-to-down and reversed left-to-right).
  - Up becomes Down (South/North flip depending on tube angle), Left becomes Right.
  - When star-hopping with sky charts: Rotate your mental map 180° when looking through the eyepiece, or consult the dedicated **Viewport B (Eyepiece Simulation)** which is pre-inverted.
- **Finder Scope Orientations**:
  - **Telrad / Red Dot Finder**: 1× magnification, upright, naked-eye orientation (matches standard wide-field star charts).
  - **Straight-Through Optical Finder (e.g. 9×50)**: Inverted 180° (matches the main Newtonian eyepiece).
  - **Right-Angle Correct-Image (RACI) Finder**: Upright and unreversed (matches standard sky charts).

## 4. Operational Best Practices
1. **Mirror Acclimation**: The 200 mm Pyrex/plate glass mirror requires **30 to 45 minutes** to reach thermal equilibrium with ambient outdoor air. Unpack the telescope before twilight so internal tube air currents dissipate before critical observing begins.
2. **Zenith Blindspot ("Dobson's Hole")**: At altitudes >80°, tracking becomes awkward because horizontal azimuth movement requires spinning the entire heavy base. Target objects around 65°–75° altitude rather than right at zenith.
3. **Collimation**: Check primary and secondary mirror alignment with a Cheshire or laser collimator prior to high-power planetary observing.
